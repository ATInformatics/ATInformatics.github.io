01

Education and experience.

Degree

BSc with Honours in Immunology and Biochemistry, University of Strathclyde, Glasgow. Graduated 2018.

Dissertation

A data analysis dissertation, part neuroscience and part computational: sleep architecture in young against old mice, and how it maps onto human sleep. I worked from EEG and EMG recordings, electrodes reading brain tissue and muscle tissue, scored the records into wake, REM and NREM, then extracted and combined those stages and fitted probability curves across several statistical methods.

Earlier schooling

Natural Sciences Academy, Bulgaria, where the chemistry and biology came from. Top of my class. I also studied German and worked as a translator for hospitals during university. The reading has stayed with me; the speaking has not.

Laboratory

A private hospital laboratory: blood work, sample handling and routine diagnostic testing. Then testing through the COVID pandemic. High volume, fixed protocols, and a result somebody acts on the same day.

Freelance data analysis

Market research for companies that did not want a full-time analyst. Working from UK government population data, I pulled the relevant fields out of very large sets, age, occupation, sex, and returned a report together with the extracted files and a written account of what I had taken and why, so the client could check it.

Software

I build and run A.T. Informatics alone: AI automation and websites for small businesses, and three products. Python backends, a REAPER integration layer, a React frontend, a native macOS app, and the deployment under all of it.

Audio and stage

I produce professionally. I have also worked in theatre, including directing a play.

Tools

Python, MATLAB, XLStat, Swift, JavaScript, SQL, Git, the Microsoft suite, web design and front-end build, and the REAPER scripting surface. German, reading.

02

What it taught me, and what I do with it.

I can take a scientific paper apart

This is what four years actually bought me, and it was not facts. I can read a difficult paper, work out what was done and why that design rather than another, and judge whether the conclusion is carried by the data or by the discussion. It is also why the AI work I find most interesting is in my own field: running pattern recognition over decades of old immunology papers to surface connections nobody made, map immune cells against disease, and point at experiments worth running.

I analyse data for people who are paying for it

The dissertation is where I learned the method. The harder version was freelance: a client, a deadline, a public dataset far too large to look at, and a report that had to record what I extracted and why, so somebody else could repeat it.

I work to a protocol, and I write them

A laboratory teaches you that a procedure which is almost right is wrong, and that a result you cannot repeat is not a result. Everything I build now reads back what it wrote instead of reporting success. One of my products reconciled 364 of 364 figures against what a client had actually filed, not against my own output.

I carry deadlines, and other people's time

Directing a play, producing records to a release date, and running a business on my own all teach the same lesson: the work is rarely the hard part, the sequencing is. Several things in flight, people waiting on each of them, and a date that does not move.

I plan the work before I write it

Agent and AI work punishes you hardest for skipping this. I turn a job into steps with the check that closes each one, and I write that down first. It is the same instinct as a method section, and it is why I can quote a fixed price instead of an estimate.

I learn a regulated domain properly

I modelled Bulgarian tax and payroll law closely enough to produce declarations a client files with the revenue office. Reading the statute is the job. The code is the easy part.

I instrument a field rather than only working in it

I implemented ITU-R BS.1770 loudness measurement and loudness-matched blind comparison from the published standard. Knowing a subject well enough to build its instruments is a different thing from being able to use them.
